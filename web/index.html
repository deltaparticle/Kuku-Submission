    Method
    
    
    The model names lines. Code does the cutting.
    
    
    An editor watches every episode to find the 30 seconds that make a stranger stop scrolling. SnipDeck
      reads the timestamped screenplay instead and proposes those cuts, ranked by what to post first.
    

    
    What a promo has to be
    
    
    Taken from the brief: 20–60 seconds, a hook inside the first three seconds, an unresolved moment at the cut, and
      clear to someone who has never seen the show. It may be one stretch or two or three moments joined in story order.
    

    
    How it works
    
    
      
      
    1. Parse. Every screenplay block becomes a line with an ID (E07_L042), a type and its start
        and end time. A cut is only allowed where no one is speaking and no sound effect is playing.
      2. 
      
    2. Story bible. One model call reads the episode summaries: who is who, which twists sell the show, which
        payoffs would spoil it. Spoiler episodes are decided by a two-out-of-three vote so they don't flip between runs.
      3. 
      
    3. Candidates. One call per episode proposes three moments — as line IDs only, never times.
      4. 
      
    4. Validate. Code turns IDs into times, keeps the hook inside the first three seconds, ends on the
        cliffhanger line and checks 20–60 s. Rejected clips go back to the model with the exact lines that would fix them.
      5. 
      
    5. Score. A judge model rates hook, cliffhanger, clarity, intensity, plot relevance and genre fit, quoting
        its evidence first. A text attention check adds what a video tool would look for: how soon someone speaks,
        motion, close-ups, dead air.
      6. 
      
    6. Rank. The judge orders the top 40 three times in shuffled order; positions are combined. Code then
        applies penalties (spoilers, ad policy), a quality bar and a limit of two promos per episode.
      7. 
      
    7. Review. An editor approves or rejects each pick here and exports the list.
      8.

    Who does what
    
    
    
      
    ModelReading the story, proposing moments, judging hooks and cliffhangers, ranking.
    
      
    CodeEvery timestamp, the 20–60 s limit, the 3-second hook, safe cuts, no overlaps, two per episode, the attention check.
    
      
    EditorFinal approve / reject, exported as a CSV that can be used to tune the weights.
    
    
    

    
    What the checks show — and what they don't
    
    
    On the 50-episode pack: all 32 picks pass the hard rules when re-checked from the saved output; against a
      keyword-based baseline, a judge model shown both clips blind preferred SnipDeck 13 times, the baseline 4 times,
      with 5 ties; an independent repeat run chose the same scene for 4 of the top 5.
    
    
    None of that is a human editor's verdict, and none of it proves a clip will perform. The judge is the same model
      family as the picker, visual hooks are read from text, and screenplay times are assumed to match the video. The
      real test is a split test on hook rate, hold rate and click-through.
